# The combat surf maps

game-g2gfast imports compiled maps from the surf genre, and some of them are not courses: small maps with buy zones, weapons on the floor and teleports back to a spawn, built to fight on. g2gfast marks them `"kind": "arena"` in its `maps/zones/<id>.json`, the importer copies the kind into each map's manifest, g2gfast keeps them out of its own rotation, and this game plays them as a deathmatch.

## Where the files come from

`maps/imported` is a link to `arena-surf-dm-maps/maps` (the `# bootstrap-link:` line in `.gitignore`). The maps lived in g2gfast-maps until 2026-10-17 and were moved out with their `.import` markers, which were written against `res://maps/imported/`, so the link must stay at that path. The game does not ship them: a server names them in `cfg/content.yml` (see [Delivered maps](#delivered-maps)). game-g2gfast is still the importer: a new or re-imported combat surf map is made there and its directory moved into arena-surf-dm-maps. `ArenaImportedMaps` scans it (and `user://maps`) for manifests whose `kind` is `arena`: discovered, never listed, so a map g2gfast re-classifies is picked up or dropped with nothing edited here. `ArenaMap.by_id` falls through to `ArenaMap.imported(id)` and `imported_ids()` sits beside `ids()`. A local import's def has no `content_id` and is at version `0.0.0-<12 hex of its manifest's md5>`, so two machines holding different imports of one map disagree at the announce instead of colliding against different brushes.

**A checkout without the link plays the three built-ins** and every check about the imports skips, saying so. That is a supported state.

## A copy of g2gfast's loader, not a dependency

`maps/arena_bsp_map.gd` is the half of g2gfast's loader arena needs: the mesh `.bin`, the lightmap through two shaders (`arena_bsp_{lightmapped,translucent}.gdshader`, opaque unless the material says translucent, because writing `ALPHA` at all moves a surface into the transparent pass), one `ConvexPolygonShape3D` per brush and one welded displacement trimesh, the spawns and the pits. `maps/arena_map_mechanics.gd` and `maps/arena_ladder_mode.gd` are copies of g2gfast's mechanics and ladder mode, in metres through `ArenaBspMap.METRES_PER_UNIT` (0.01905).

A game cannot name another game's script and a delivered pack cannot resolve a `class_name`, so these are preloaded by path. Lifting the loader into an addon is the right home eventually; it would put it in the client shell (so arena could not use it until the addon is tagged and the shell rebuilt), and the reusable half is tangled with g2gfast's genre units, timer zones, textures and lighting. Duplicated deliberately, per the family rule.

A surface with a texture draws it; anything else draws `ArenaMap.dev_texture`, one square to 64 units, tinted the colour the map's compiler measured (black only when the material is named black, g2gfast's rule). Water with no texture draws with `arena_bsp_water.gdshader` (translucent, both sides, a slow ripple). Surfaces are unshaded and lit by their own lightmap.

## Lit from the manifest

`ArenaMapLighting.build` lights an imported map from its manifest's `lighting` block through `DotLightRig`, as game-g2gfast does (`G2GLighting`, copied rather than named): the map's sun angle and colour, its ambient, its fog, and a procedural sky from those numbers, continued under the horizon (`imported_profile`, a stock tier, shadows off in a browser). Three of g2gfast's decisions come with it. **A white ambient is the editor's default, not a sky** (ten of the thirty-three maps): the zenith is looked up by sky name in `SKY_TOPS` and put in the document as `sky_top` (`imported_document`). **A sky named black is black all round.** **A map that packed its six sky faces draws them** through `arena_bsp_sky.gdshader` (a copy of g2gfast's; eight maps). **The lightmap's shade is lifted** (`arena_bsp_lightmapped.gdshader`, `lift` 0.55 as a gamma and `ambient` 0.20, g2gfast had no gamma and 0.10): most maps' median lightmap texel is a fifth of full, and rendered on a real GPU surf_dust2_2008_final's floor and surf_greatriver's were near black under a bright sky; white (a fullbright map) stays white, checked on surf_pox and surf_10x_final. A map whose textures are themselves dark (greatriver) stays a dark map. Sky gradient, SSIL and a stronger SSAO on the built-in maps were tried (Forward+, GTX 1660 Ti) and dropped: a wider `sky_curve` washed the zenith out and the occlusion changed nothing visible. The textures and the lightmap are otherwise untouched: the sun lights the players, and the sky and the fog are what change around the map. A manifest that does not read gets the old fixed sun and grey-blue (`_fixed`), with a WARN.

**`sky_top` is drawn by dot-lighting** (API level 2; the pack asks for 3, for the built-in maps' `painted()`, so there is no older path to repaint for). `headless_imported`'s lighting section checks it, and `tools/screenshot.sh` draws an imported map under exactly this lighting.

## The solid is the game's, on every machine

`ArenaGame._build_world_collision` adds `ArenaMap.to_collision()` (the brushes) as `WorldCollision` before the combat manager and before any player is built or re-bodied, on setup and on every `change_map`, and frees it with `free()`, not `queue_free()`, when the map goes. `ArenaMap.movement_body()` and `shot_trace()` bind to it (`DotFpsPhysicsBody`, `DotTracePhysics`); a built-in map still gets `to_fps_body()` and `to_trace()`. `to_scene()` of an imported map is the drawn mesh only, so a client never has two copies of a brush. Both ends query the same convex hulls through the same solver, which is the condition the prediction rule is about ([netcode](netcode.md#the-client-predicts-with-the-servers-collision)); the body is still a shape query from a plain `Node3D`.

**Arena needs dot-player-controller v0.1.5 or later** (in client shell 20261009-1627 and after): `DotFpsFlatBody.rest_contact` used to push a player out of a box along its deepest axis, and with the 512 m extent's quantisation a client slid 2.8 m along a wall during a launch the server made straight up.

## Bots route over a mesh baked from the brushes

The navigation graph `ArenaNpcs.nav_for` lays is a grid over a box list, which an imported map does not have, so `ArenaBots.World.route` asks dot-bot's `DotBotNav` instead: baked from `collision_root` with the body's radius, standing height and step, once per map, on the first route a bot asks for. It bakes with `bake_in_background`, because the two default surf maps took 2.2 and 2.8 s against a map change of 30 to 130 ms; a coarser cell only brought that to 0.5 s. Until it lands `route` answers empty and bots walk straight, as they did before. A combat surf map is two halves joined by ramps and drops, which a walking mesh does not hold: a route to the far half ends at the edge of the near one and the brain walks straight on from there. `headless_imported`'s "bots route round the brushes" section checks every leg from one spawn to every other is clear, the near half is reached in full and the far half's routes end nearer than they start. **The mesh is baked from `ArenaMap.nav_source()`, not `collision_root` itself**: a mapper's sealed bounding box (six slabs, 500 m by 430 m and 60 cm thick) made Recast find nowhere to walk at all (0 polygons of 63,000 vertices; 7,400 once the slabs were left out), so a hull that is under 1.5 m thick and over 150 m in its second-longest dimension is left out of the bake (the players still collide with it). Maps without such a slab bake `collision_root` as before. Because the mesh lands when the machine finishes it, bots on an imported map are not deterministic run to run; built-in maps still are.

## Spawns and teleports are settled at load

Mapper spawns and pit destinations are often inside solid, and the motor pushes out at most a step a tick, so a player rose through the floor. `ArenaMap.settle_spawns` lifts each one in 5 cm steps until a standing hull is clear, up to 2 m, before `_build_match` makes spawn points of them.

## Pits are teleports, and the floor under the map catches

`ArenaPlayer._follow_map_volumes` runs inside the simulated tick, on the server and in the owning client's prediction alike, so neither end has to tell the other: feet entering a RESPAWN zone are put at its destination facing its yaw, and a player 10 m under the map's bounds is put back at a spawn.

## The jail

A map whose teleport volumes make a button (a volume that other pits aim into and that itself aims outside every volume) with exits near its destination gets an `ArenaJail` (`maps/arena_jail.gd`), found by shape and never by name. While `arena_jail` is 1: a fall puts the player in the cell, standing in the button volume presses it, and the exits work only while the gate is open (`arena_jail_open` seconds), after which the gate stays shut for `arena_jail_cooldown` seconds. Shut, an exit puts the player back in the cell, so the gate needs no collision toggling. The state is a pure function of the tick and `opened_at`, which clients are told in a JAIL event; `ArenaPlayer` asks `ArenaMap.pit_at(feet, tick)` in the simulated tick on both ends. `ArenaJailView` draws the gate and the button, and is presentation only. Turned off, every volume does what the map says.

**surf_ski_2's catch-all floor teleport aims at a roofed cell, not a ramp.** Its only way out was a door the map's button opens; the importer runs no outputs, so the door stayed shut and a faller (a bot, above all) was stuck. The map's zones file now lists it under `open_doors` (game-g2gfast, `maps/zones/README.md`), and `headless_imported`'s fall-out section walks a faller out of the cell. The map has no `ArenaJail`: its button is a physical `func_button`, not a teleport volume. The same section also probes the biggest destinations of every combat surf map (`_test_fallout_all`: grounded travel only, 12 m or a 3 m drop counts as out), though it was never seen failing on the old shut-door data, so treat it as a smoke check; the sharp detector is game-g2gfast's `tools/teleport_traps.py` on the source BSPs. `tools/render_fallout.sh <map> [rank]` photographs a destination (needs xvfb).

## The surf genre's air control on these maps

`ArenaPlayer.SURF_TUNABLES` is `air_accelerate` 150 (what g2gfast runs them at), a 30 u/s wish cap, 800 u/s² of gravity, a 57-unit jump and the 3500 u/s limit; ground movement, slide, launch and dash stay the arena's. `ArenaGame.map_movement()` is derived on both ends from `ArenaMap.movement_profile` (`&"surf"` for a `kind: arena` map), and `ArenaPlayer.apply_map_movement` writes it and puts every key it is not given back to `arena_tunables()`, which is what takes it off on the next built-in map. It is applied before a player enters the tree and before `rebind_map`. `arena_surf` (1/0) and `arena_surf_airaccelerate` (150) ride RULES because a client predicts with them. Christian's pick is the default; the arena's own movement is one cvar away.

**The hull stays the arena's 1.8 m**, not the genre's 1.37 m, because the hitboxes and avatars are built for it. Every spawn room and doorway checked fits it; a genre-sized vent somewhere would not.

## Bots ride the ramps

A combat surf map's walking mesh is islands: platforms with ramps between them that nobody can stand on. `game/arena_surf.gd` adds **ride edges** to it, one-way navigation links from one platform to another that a ramp carries a player across, so a route may cross one; when the brain's route reaches an edge's start (`DotBotIntent.ride`) the legs go to dot-bot's surf pilot (`DotBotSurfRide`) until it lands, while the brain keeps aiming and shooting. The map's small teleports are walked links too (a bot at the foot of a ramp walks into the one that takes it back up), and every teleport is a pit on every ride. The landings are waypoints, so roaming bots ride of their own accord. dot-bot's `docs/surfing.md` has the search, the cache and the pilot; the family's study is `game-dev/docs/surf-bots-study.md`.

- **Learned offline, never at map load.** `tools/surf_rides.tscn -- <map> --explore` bakes the mesh exactly as the game does and searches, on the real motor (dot-player-controller's `DotFpsSimulator`), from every spawn platform toward every platform at least 40 m away and lower, lowest first, never into a pit; each ride found is an edge, to the island it actually landed on, written as it is found (`--platforms n` caps the spawn platforms tried, `--islands` lists them, `--picture` draws them). `--plan i,j` plans those edges (rides from six perturbed starts, then repair rounds), `--measure` rides them simulated and in the game with the view held elsewhere. The result is `maps/surf/<id>.surf.json`; replan after a movement or collision change.
- **A course is "standing on the landing platform"** (`ArenaSurf.course_for`: grounded over the island, `DotBotNav.on_island`), and two course settings dot-bot added for these maps: a state with nothing under it or ahead of it but the floor teleport scores 150 m less (`unsupported_test`), and progress is how much nearer the landing a state is, never past it (`toward_goal`). Without them every line of the search fell off the ramp toward the goal and outscored the riders until it reached the floor.
- **The simulator copies the arena's body**: the map's own movement body (never the solid-players wrapper, whose other players would stand still in a forecast), the riding player's tunables, the map's pushes as its mechanics. One per edge, shared by every bot riding it.
- **Cost.** The pilot keeps its 192-tick forecast and extends it a tick at a time while the ride stays on it, so a ride that goes as foreseen costs one simulated tick per ridden tick plus the lookup; a veto (every action's future) is the rare expensive tick. Measured in `tools/surf_rides.tscn --measure`.
- **Recorded rides.** Off by default (`bot_record 0`; `bot_record_*` cvars and `user://bot_record.json`). When on, `game/arena_surf_rides.gd` records real players' rides between platforms (a bot is never recorded), replays them on the motor a slice a frame, and a bot on that edge runs up with the pilot and plays a verified recording from the takeoff. `tools/surf_rides.tscn -- <map> --demos` lists what is stored, `--harvest` verifies it. Needs a dot-bot with the recorder stores (the API level does not say so yet).
- **Older shells.** `arena_bots.gd` loads `arena_surf.gd` by path only when the shell has dot-bot 4 and dot-player-controller 3; otherwise bots walk as before.
- **The view on a ride.** In the air the bot looks where the brain aims (and shoots); on the ground and on a jump it looks along the ride, because a backward key is slower and the arena's wall jump pushes off along the view. The arena's wall moves still probe walls partly along the view, so a ride made looking elsewhere lands less often than one looking along it.
- **Measured** (surf_ski_2, `tools/surf_rides.tscn --measure`, perturbed running starts): edge 1 (82 m, 4.3 to 4.6 s) lands 88 to 90% simulated and 90% live with the view on the map's middle, 100% of six driven through `ArenaBots.think`; edge 7 (96 m) 72 to 80% simulated, 80% live, 50% through the brain; edge 3 22 to 40% simulated (its landing is a step from edge 1's, and the repair rounds did not close it). A ride that goes as foreseen costs about 0.45 ms of steering a tick at 64 Hz (3% of a core per riding bot, 1.2 to 1.6 simulated ticks a ridden tick); one that vetoes costs 2 to 4 ms while it does; planning took 6, 31 and 14 minutes on one core.
- **Which maps have rides.** surf_ski_2: three edges planned of eight found (`headless_imported`'s first ride section plays it). surf_10x_reloaded_fixed: six edges. Five found by `--explore` (lowest landing first, at least 40 m away, `--platforms 2`), all from a spawn room down the -x ramp to the y -81 floor round the middle (280 to 312 m, 9 to 12 s); the sixth added with `--search 192,61,19 0,0,0 22,19 --width 32 --seconds 30 --add` (an 11.5 s ride from the spawn platform down the -x ramp and the upper chain to the far island 22, 414 m; `--add` appends the found ride as an edge of the file, for a platform `--explore` never reached; islands 19 to 21 are the same far platform's neighbours and a landing on any counts). Planned: edge 0 (280 m: 75% of 40 simulated, 55% of 20 live), edge 2 (312 m: 72% simulated, 45% live; 22 min) and edge 5 (to island 22: 45% simulated, 40% live; 27 min, 10 rides from 117 searches). Most misses clip a teleport near the landing (or the one at the spawn room's door) and come back at spawn; steering cost 4 to 12 ms a riding tick (dot-bot's pilot scanned every cache entry for a state off every ride) and is now 1.0 to 1.3 ms (a coarse grid for that case), landing rates the same (live, 12 rides: edge 0 75%, edge 2 58%, edge 5 50%; simulated 60, 80, 45). Edges 1, 3 and 4 are found, not planned. `headless_imported`'s rotation section rides the three planned edges from six starts each and needs two to land. surf_110b_austinpowers has none, and the measurements say there is none to find: 41 of its 193 islands are teleport floors, every room's floor sends a player to a holding room that sends them back to spawn, and its big islands are roofs above the ride. Twenty-four searches from both spawn platforms, searches toward each candidate landing, a width-128 45 s search and searches chained from mid-ride all ended in a pit within 3.4 to 11.1 s; the furthest went about 265 m through three rooms. It is a ride from spawn to a teleport, not from platform to platform. surf_snipers_island: three edges, found with `--search` rather than `--explore`, because `--explore` starts only from platforms of at least 12 m² and this map is one big island (the spawns) with three small pads on its ramps that its teleports put a player on (islands 54 to 56); it found only the degenerate 0.14 s ride from island 2 to itself, which is not kept. `--search <pad> 0,0,0 2 --width 32 --seconds 15 --add` from each teleport landing found the ride down to the spawn island (0.9 to 1.7 s, 64 to 107 m), then `--plan 0,1,2` (a few seconds each). `--measure --runs 40 --live 20`: 100% simulated and 100% live on every edge. The way up is the walked teleport link, so a bot roams spawn island, teleport, pad, ride, and `headless_imported` checks both halves (three edges ridden from six starts, five needed; a roamer from a spawn is handed a ride by its own route). Two things the roaming needed, both found by that check failing: the roaming waypoints thin a map's spawns (48 on one island swamped the few places a ride is taken from; `ArenaBots.World.waypoints`) and add the teleport doors that lead to a ride's pad (`ArenaSurf.landing_points`; a route to a point on a pad ends on the floor beneath it, never through the teleport's link), and `ArenaBots.think` tells the brain of a teleport (more than 4 m in a tick, no ride under way) with `ride_ended`, because its route was laid from where the bot stood and walking its old corners off the pad it landed on was a fall, never a ride. The `--explore` verdict stands: it found nothing here, the rides are the pads'. surf_the_flow: two edges, one from each spawn platform (16 spawns each, y 43) down to the floor island the four teleports land on (3.2 and 3.5 s), found by `--explore --platforms 2` in 6 s each and planned with `--plan 0,1` (41 s an edge, 0 of 32 repair rides failed). `--measure --runs 40 --live 20`: 100% simulated on both, live 95% (19 of 20) and 100%, steering 220 us a riding tick. The way up is the teleports' own pits, so the ride is the only half a bot needs; `headless_imported`'s rotation section rides both from six starts (five needed) and checks a roamer from a spawn is handed one.

## Rotation and modes

`arena_imported_rotation` (`all`, `none`, or ids) defaults to `all`: g2gfast keeps every `kind: arena` map out of its rotation, so every one is offered here by the rotation, the ballot and nominations (a map left out by the setting is `enabled = false`, still loadable with `arena_map <id>`). `ArenaMaps.supports_mode` keeps siege, the objective modes and every props mode off them (`needs_built_world`: navigation, objective layout and scattered props are built from a box list), and their untagged spawns keep the team modes off. Free-for-all, gun game and the weapon pools play.

## Delivered maps

A server names its combat surf maps the way every game does: `ArenaMapDirector.server` is the dot-server, `ArenaMaps.server_map_keys` reads `games.current_maps()` (the descriptor's `maps`, which dot-server-deploy's `cfg/content.yml` fills; duck-typed, so no list is the old behaviour), and `ArenaMaps.adopt_delivered` puts each `<owner>/<id>@<version>` in the catalogue as delivered content without fetching it (they are megabytes each). `game.yml` has no `maps:` on purpose: the server owner names them.

- `ArenaMapSession._mount_delivered` fetches the pack on the change (`DotMapLoader.ensure_content`; arena does not link dot-game) and registers the mount with `ArenaImportedMaps.add_mounted(id, dir)`. A mount holds `<id>.json` directly, so it is registered per id rather than scanned as a root. It is not in `ids()`, and it wins over a link copy of the same id, because both ends must build the pack's bytes.
- On a client the same session method runs from `DotMapSyncClient`'s load. A client with the link drops its local def for that id first (`ArenaClientExtras._prefer_delivered`), or dot-map refuses the announce as a different version.
- **A delivered map's version is the pack's**, so server and client agree by construction; the md5 version is for local imports only.
- Only `"kind": "arena"` manifests play: one on disk that says otherwise is skipped, one fetched on a change is refused and leaves the catalogue. A key with a built-in id is skipped. `director.adopt_server_maps()` re-reads the list; the running map keeps its mount.

**Naming them is the server owner's, and publishing is a tag of arena-surf-dm-maps.** The ready-to-paste `cfg/content.yml` block for every map is in that repository's README ("Naming them on a server"); dot-server-deploy's own `cfg/content.yml` has no `game-arena` entry yet, and a map added after the last tag (v1.0.1) is not a pack until the next. Not covered by a suite: a real client fetching a real pack over the wire, and `_prefer_delivered`.

## The maps' own mechanics

Pushes and boosters, water, ladders, conveyors, gravity and hurt volumes from each manifest's `mechanics` block run here. `ArenaMap.imported` reads it into `ArenaMap.mechanics`, null on a built-in map, and that null is how every caller knows there is nothing to run.

- **Inside the simulated tick, on both ends.** `ArenaPlayer.simulate_tick` runs `_run_map_mechanics` after the controller's move and before `_follow_map_volumes`: pushes, then gravity or a conveyor, then the ladder, then water (a ladder wins over water), then the pits. The node is moved after a push so the muzzle and hitboxes follow.
- **The swim and ladder modes are registered on every player on every map** (`controller.extra_modes = [swim, ladder]`, in that order), because a mode's id travels in the state's flags and a client that registered them only on a map with water would misread a swimmer's mode. With no volumes neither is entered and a built-in map moves exactly as before.
- **Pushes are stateless.** g2gfast keys a push history by `DotFpsState.tick`, which a rewind does not restore. Here "was this player in it last tick" is asked of the hull where the tick started, and a push that displaces a player out of its box hands its velocity over on that same tick, or a floor booster's own displacement carries the player off its end before it throws. (Whether g2gfast's history mispredicts a booster on a connected client is unchecked.)
- **The ladder's jump-off grace counts the game's tick** (`ArenaLadderMode.now`, written by the player), for the same reason.
- **Hurt is real damage.** `ArenaGame._hurt_from_map` deals `damage` per second every half second (`ArenaMapMechanics.HURT_INTERVAL`), the first pulse on entering, through `DotCombatManager.apply_damage` as attacker 0 with the `world` type, on the authority only, after the shots and before the match. A negative amount heals. Not predicted: health is the server's.
- **Sinking blocks are not ported**: none of the maps has one, and they are a timer map's mechanic. `ArenaMapMechanics.blocks_skipped` counts them so a map that gains one says so in `describe()`.
- The water is the genre's (200 u/s, a 60 u/s idle sink, so a pit full of water is climbed out of by holding jump), with waist and float depths as the genre's proportions of the 1.8 m hull.

Most of these maps' water is a few centimetres over a pit, and most hurt volumes sit over a teleport, so checks look for the deep pool and the pit-free volumes rather than the first one found.
