# Weapons: arena-weapons, set and runtime

**The weapon set and its runtime are arena-weapons'** (`addons/arena_weapons`, a required link; game-arena links no other weapon addon): its `AwWeaponPack` builds every definition (knife; pistol, revolver, machine pistol, derringer; SMG, carbine, rifle, bullpup, battle rifle, burst rifle, shotgun, drum shotgun, marksman, LMG; sniper, minigun, launcher, quad launcher, grenade launcher, plasma beamer, rail rifle; frag, sticky), its `AwWeaponArt` the model and holds of each, and its `Aw*` runtime (rig, view model, world model, shot effects, art registry, damage table, ADS rules in `cfg/addon/arena_weapons/ads.yml`) draws and runs them. `ArenaWeaponRoles.scan` always registers it, first and under plain ids (`set_id_prefix("")`); without it the arena has no weapons and says so with an ERROR. Wire numbers are `AwWeaponIds.all()` (append-only, the old index unchanged), then any other pack's ids. A loadout a client remembered from before (`aw_rifle`, or a retired melee weapon) is mapped by `ArenaWeaponRoles.alias` through dot-loadout's `item_alias_fn`. Every stat is tunable per weapon in `cfg/addons/arena_weapons/weapon/<id>.yml` (dot-weapon's profiles; arena-weapons' own `cfg/` holds the builder numbers to copy from). The damage table is the pack's types plus this game's `bullet` (the horde's gunner), `fall` and `world`, built once and handed to the pack and to dot-combat. The base stand-in art (knife, pistol, rifle, launcher, frag rows of `AwWeaponArtTable`) still points at CC0 models vendored in `assets/`. `ArenaWeaponSkins` dresses base art by role for a pack that only skins (`role_art()`); with the own pack registered it has nothing to dress. `tools/render_weapons.sh` renders a hip and an aim frame of every weapon into `screenshots/weapons/`.


**Weapons by role.** `ArenaWeaponRoles` (`game/arena_weapon_roles.gd`) answers which weapon fills melee, secondary, primary, heavy and thrown, and which packs add weapons: any addon with `spawn_provider.gd` and a static `weapon_defs()` (the contract Zombie Zone scans too) joins the catalogue after arena-weapons through `AwWeaponCatalogue.add_source` and `AwWeaponArtTable.add_source`. `weapons.packs: [zz_modern_weapons]` in `cfg/addon/game_arena_weapons/*.yml` (dot-core `DotAddonConfig`) adds another pack; `weapons.roles` pins a role to an id, `weapons.exclude` drops ids, `--arena-no-weapon-packs` drops every extra pack (the own pack stays). A role's automatic pick is the pack weapon of that arsenal slot. Extra pack weapons take wire numbers after the own pack's (`WEAPON_INDEX_BITS` is 6 for that), and a pack without art is dressed from its `art_rows()` over the base gun of its slot. `headless_roles` holds it.

## The rig drives the player's own arsenal

`ArenaPlayer._build_rig` hands a `AwWeaponRig` the existing `Arsenal` node by `arsenal_ref` rather than letting it build its own. A rig's role, which decides what it draws, is fixed at `setup()`, and a player is built before anybody knows whether it is the one at this keyboard. So the client rebuilds the rig as LOCAL when it adopts its player (`arm_first_person`, with a `AwViewModel` under the camera), and everything carried survives because it lives in the arsenal. Every reader of `player.arsenal` (HUD, netcode, mod tools, loadout) reads the same node. The old rig is freed with `free()`, not `queue_free()`: one alive until the end of the frame counts a reload twice.

- Simulation goes through `weapons.simulate_tick`, which runs the arsenal and then the bash.
- `used` is the rig's signal, so it carries the bash and is silent in a replay ([netcode](netcode.md#weapons-over-the-wire)).
- Somebody else's gun is a `AwWorldModel` on the hand `ArenaPlayer.attachment()` answers for (the person's own gun hand, `DotHumanoidFigure.attachment`; a capsule gets a fixed point), driven from the snapshot on a mirror and from the rig on anything this machine simulates, on one code path.
- The camera's half of the recoil is added in `present`, through `has_method`: `view_punch` and `shot_fx` are newer than some client shells' copy of arena-weapons, and a call by name to a method the shell's copy lacks would not compile. On such a shell the game runs with no punch and with arena's own flash and report.

## Projectiles

`ArenaProjectiles` separates a fused spawn (bounces, rolls to rest, sticks if the definition says `sticks`, goes off on the fuse) from a contact one (the launcher). A grenade cooked past its fuse goes off at once. The thrower is ignored for eight ticks, because a grenade lobbed while running is overtaken by its own thrower. `ArenaProjectileView` draws the list; `ArenaPresentation.on_explosion` plays `scenes/fx/explosion.tscn` and a shake that falls off with distance. A resting grenade carries a red fuse light, because at range it is two brown pixels.

**Utility grenades** (flash, smoke, incendiary; arena_weapons level 3) are `game/arena_grenades.gd`, listening to `ArenaProjectiles.detonated` on the server. `AwGrenadeBlast.detonate` decides who is blinded and what cloud or fire appears; the fire zone is pulsed every half second through `combat.apply_damage`, so friendly fire and self damage are the rules every other hit follows. Flight, bounce and the thrower grace stay in `ArenaProjectiles` (a def with `explode_on_impact`, the incendiary, goes off on first contact); `AwGrenade.step` is not used. `sv_grenade_*` are arena_weapons' `AwGrenadeRules`; **`sv_grenade_utility` (default 0)** entitles everyone to the three in the loadout menu, because the items are not free. A flash blinds and smoke blocks the sight of player bots (dot-bot level 8) and of horde monsters (dot-npc level 6, both guarded by `build_level`); hearing passes through smoke. `examples/headless_grenades.gd` is the suite.

**A projectile passes through a team-mate who holds its launch point**, as a gun's shot does in dot-combat: `ArenaProjectiles` asks `DotCombatManager.pass_through_sets` for the launch point once, and the flight sweep leaves those bodies out for the thrower's grace and for as long as the projectile is still inside them. Team-mates do not collide, so two can stand in one place, and the sweep used to exclude only the thrower: every rocket from inside a friend went off in them at 0 m with its splash on the shooter, and a grenade bounced off them. The policy is dot-combat's (`DotDamageRules.pass_through_team_mate_at_muzzle`, `pass_through_fn`); an enemy holding the launch point is still hit at 0 m. Called by name, so a host whose dot-combat predates the method loads arena and keeps the old behaviour.

**The server's `mp_teammate_passthrough` reaches projectiles too.** It is dot-combat's cvar (`DotCombatManager.bind_cvars`, bound by name in `arena_module.gd`): 0 turns the launch-point rule off, 1 is the above, and 2 (`DotDamageRules.pass_through_team_mates`) makes `pass_through_sets` answer every team-mate, which `ArenaProjectiles` keeps out for the whole flight (`Flying.pass_all`) rather than dropping each once the projectile has left it. A rocket then flies past a friend in a corridor to the enemy behind them; its blast is not a sweep and still reaches team-mates under friendly fire.

**A detonation pins the attacker's believed origin to the blast** for that one resolution (`ArenaProjectiles.origin_of`) and puts it back. `DotCombatManager.resolve_shot` corrects any shot whose origin is more than `max_origin_error` from where it believes the attacker is (right for a gun) and re-traces; for a blast that put every explosion in its owner's face. Any check about explosives must ask the thrower's health too.

## Loadouts

- **A spawn hands over the heaviest gun** (`_better_hand`), not the highest slot, which is the throwable in the pack's layout.
- **Melee and the throwable are required slots.** dot-loadout's `default_loadout()` fills required slots only, so optional ones vanished a frame after every spawn. A loadout saved before they existed is filled in on load rather than refused.


The loadout menu's pictures are the models rendered at 512x256 with MSAA (arena-weapons' `AwWeaponPreview`, cached per weapon), not the sprite sheet upscaled; a weapon without a model keeps the sprite.

## Recoil

Every firearm kicks, and the numbers live in the Dot profile, not in code.

- **Where.** `cfg/game/game-arena/weapon/<id>.yml` (the game tier; `ArenaContent.weapons()` sets `AwWeaponPack.game`). Only the changed keys are listed, under `extra: tuning:`: `recoil_pitch` and `recoil_yaw` (degrees per shot; yaw alternates and drifts by a per-weapon seed, `recoil_seed`), `recoil_build` / `recoil_build_max` (the extra kick along a burst), `recoil_recovery` (how fast it settles, per second), `recoil_aim` (ADS multiplier, -1 uses `sv_recoil_mult_aim`), `recoil_crouch`, `recoil_move`, `recoil_camera`. Grenades, the knife and the beamer do not kick. An operator overrides a weapon in `cfg/server/...`.
- **Server cvars** (replicated, `sv_recoil_*`): `enabled`, `scale` (0 disables), `vertical`, `horizontal`, `recovery`, `build`, `punch`, `follow`, `reset`, `mult_aim`, `mult_crouch`, `mult_move`, `mult_air`.
- **Client cvar** `cl_recoil_view` (0..5, default 1): how far the drawn camera follows the kick (the weapon pose's own camera spring supplies 30% of it, the recoil view the other 70%, so the total matches the bullet's climb). 0 keeps the crosshair still; the bullets still climb.
- **Prediction.** The bullet's offset is a pure function of the weapon's burst state, so the server and a predicting client compute the same shot. The camera half (`ArenaRecoilView`) is fed by the rig's `used` signal, which is silent while a correction replays, decays at the same recovery rate and is added only where the camera is written, never to the command angles. `cl_recoil_view` therefore changes no simulated state.
- **Spray by class.** A held second (server rules, no compensation) climbs about 1.5 to 4 degrees on the automatics, more on the rifle and lmg than the smg, and wanders sideways by the yaw drift; single-shot weapons kick once, hard (revolver, marksman, sniper). Everything settles within a second and a half of release. `headless_recoil` prints the table.
- **Killcam.** The recorded look is the killer's aim and carries none of the camera's recoil, so the replay rebuilds it: every step of the recorded fire count kicks the replay camera through the same `sv_recoil_*` rules and the weapon's tuning (`ArenaKillPovRecoil`), several rounds in one frame kick several times, and a recorded signed kick is used as it was. Client cvar `cl_killcam_recoil` (0..5, default 1) scales it; 0 holds the replay still.
- **Test.** `examples/headless_recoil.tscn`.

## Hit zones, damage and bot fairness

Measured by `examples/headless_hitzones.tscn` (one round of every firearm on each zone at 10 m, and the real bot brain at each difficulty).

- **Same for everyone.** Bots and players share one hitbox build (head sphere r 0.15 at 1.62 m, neck, torso, legs, feet, scaled by the body scale) and one damage resolver: the zone multipliers, falloff and the 400 cap apply to every attacker alike. There is no bot-only damage or hitbox.
- **Why a rifle "one-shots".** A spawn has 100 health and **no armour** (armour comes only from a loadout item or a streak). The head multiplier is 4, so one rifle round to the head takes 92 and leaves 8; any chip damage, or the next round of the same burst, kills. With 100 armour the same round takes 46. No automatic and not the pistol kills a fresh player with one round anywhere; the revolver, battle rifle, derringer and sniper do to the head, as intended.
- **Bots held recoil perfectly.** A bot's aim is its command, so it used to pull against the full recoil of the next shot: a laser at every difficulty, which is what put two rifle rounds on one head. Now it pulls against a share by difficulty: easy 0.3, medium 0.55, hard 0.75, nightmare 0.9 (`ArenaBots.RECOIL_CONTROL`). Server cvar `bot_recoil_control` (-1..1, default -1 = by difficulty) sets one share for every bot.
- **Hit zone multipliers** are server cvars (archived, notified): `arena_head_mult` (default 4), `arena_neck_mult` (2), `arena_stomach_mult` (1.25), `arena_leg_mult` (0.75). Chest is 1, arms 0.75, hands and feet 0.5.
- **Bot accuracy by difficulty** (rifle, 12 m, 12 s): landed share against a still target goes from about a quarter on easy, medium and hard to about three quarters on nightmare; against a strafing target, about 10 to 15% up to under half. Head share of hits stays near 10 to 15%, since bots aim at the chest. Easy, medium and hard differ more in reaction and tracking (dot-bot's presets) than in a still target's hit share.
